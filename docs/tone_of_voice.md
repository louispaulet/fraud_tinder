# Tone of Voice

Website copy should sound like a sharp but friendly inspection bureau clerk: observant, a little theatrical, and never cruel.

The tone should support the game's "Papers, Please" inspiration without becoming grim, bureaucratic sludge. The player should feel clever and curious, not scolded.

## Core Qualities

- Clear: explain what the player can do and what just happened.
- Playful: use light inspection-desk flavor.
- Respectful: never shame players for wrong answers.
- Specific: name the clue, risk, or reason.
- Synthetic-aware: keep reminders that examples are fictional where relevant.

## Voice Examples

Use copy like:

- "The first file is waiting on your desk."
- "Left stamps a scam. Right clears the file."
- "Correct classification."
- "Missed signal."
- "This discount is doing a little too much."
- "The seller wants a private checkout, which removes buyer protection."

Avoid copy like:

- "You failed."
- "Obviously fake."
- "Only an idiot would click this."
- "Real eBay scam found online."
- "This person got robbed."

## Scam Feedback

When explaining a scam, focus on the signal:

- "The payment request moves the buyer away from platform protection."
- "The countdown creates urgency before verification."
- "The tiny fee is a common way to collect card details."

Do not imply that all cheap listings, all new sellers, or all delivery fees are scams. Phrase explanations as combinations of clues.

## Not-Scam Feedback

When explaining a not-scam case, avoid saying it is guaranteed safe. Use modest confidence:

- "This looks ordinary."
- "The price, seller history, and payment flow fit the item."
- "Nothing here pressures the reader or asks for sensitive information."

Avoid absolute claims like:

- "This is definitely safe."
- "You can trust this seller."
- "No risk here."

## UI Labels

Primary labels should stay short and decisive:

- `Scam`
- `Not scam`
- `Case ledger`
- `Inspection board`
- `Final stamp`
- `Reopen desk`

Flavor text can be more playful, but controls must remain immediately understandable.

## Accessibility and Clarity

Never let flavor obscure function. If a phrase is cute but unclear, replace it with clearer copy.

For critical actions, pair flavor with plain language:

- Good: "Swipe left: Scam"
- Good: "Reopen desk"
- Risky: "Return to the bureau"

The player should always know what a button or key will do.
